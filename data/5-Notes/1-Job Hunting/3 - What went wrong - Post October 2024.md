
The twelve-month job search following my departure from Makpar Corporation was a perfect storm of macroeconomic factors, technological disruption, and systemic hiring inefficiencies that converged to make landing a role as a Senior Full Stack Engineer exceptionally difficult, despite 25+ years of industry experience and a proven track record across multiple technical stacks and leadership roles.

---
# Post-COVID Correction

During COVID-19, tech companies massively over-hired, anticipating sustained hyper-growth
By the time my contract ended with SBA, and my release from Makpar; Companies were already correcting the over-hired by letting people go. 

# Government Contract Collapse

Government budgets, already constrained, faced additional pressure under the Trump administration's spending cuts
SBA contracts, a reliable source of technical work, began to contract
When the Makpar contract concluded in October 2024, there were no internal projects to transition to and had no choice but to let me go. This wasn't a performance issue—it was a structural collapse in contract-dependent hiring.

# The AI Disruption & Uncertainty in Tech

Artificial intelligence fundamentally shifted how companies viewed software development capacity.
ChatGPT, Claude, and other LLMs became mainstream tools in development workflows
Companies realized that fewer engineers with AI assistance could accomplish what previously required larger teams
Uncertainty plagued hiring decisions: *If AI is the future, what skills matter? What should we hire for?*

**The Hiring Freeze:** This uncertainty translated directly into a hiring freeze. Companies paused full-stack engineer roles to reassess their technical strategy, leaving the talent market in limbo 

# The Great Layoff Wave & Applicant Explosion

The convergence of these factors triggered unprecedented mass layoffs across the industry.
**January 2024 – October 2024:** Tech industry layoffs exceeded 250,000+ workers
Every senior engineer, mid-level developer, and junior programmer who lost their job suddenly entered the same job market
The candidate pool for any posted role exploded from 50–100 applicants to 500–2,000+ applicants per position

**The Compounding Effect:** My competition wasn't just skilled developers—it was *experienced* developers, many with PhDs, certifications, and specialized expertise, all applying for the same roles simultaneously.

# AI-Driven Hiring Process Breakdowns

As the applicant flood overwhelmed hiring teams, **companies turned to AI and Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS)** to filter submissions.

**What This Meant for Candidates:**

| **Hiring Challenge**    | **Company's AI Solution**                 | **My Experience**                                                                                      |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1,000+ resumes per role | ATS algorithms filter by keyword matching | Resume keywords misaligned; years of experience marked as "not relevant"                               |
| Overwhelmed recruiters  | AI screening of initial applications      | My background flagged as overqualified or too niche (SBA contract work)                                |
| Resume fatigue          | Automated rejection for formatting issues | Subtle resume formatting—lack of specific buzzwords—resulted in auto-rejection                         |
| Skill inflation         | ATS matching exact tech stack keywords    | My 25 years didn't matter; ATS wanted "3 years React hooks" not "React experience across 15+ projects" |

**The ATS Problem:**
- ATS systems matched buzzwords, not real competency
- A 2-year-old with a full-stack title could score higher than a 25-year veteran if their resume hit the right keywords
- My extensive experience across **PHP (Laravel, Laminas, Zend), Python, React, Vue.js, AWS serverless, Docker, and CI/CD** became a liability—too broad, not specialized enough for ATS parsing
- Worse: Job descriptions called for "Senior Full Stack Engineers" but ATS was tuned to reject anyone with more than 10 years of experience (potential flight risk, salary expectations)

# The Applicant-Side AI Arms Race

Desperate to break through ATS filters, **job seekers turned to AI themselves.**

**What Candidates Were Doing:**
- **Automated Bulk Applications:** Tools scraping job boards and auto-applying to 100+ roles per day using tailored AI-generated resumes
- **Resume Optimization:** AI tools analyzing ATS algorithms and rewriting resumes to maximize keyword matching, even if it made descriptions less accurate
- **Interview Prep Bots:** AI systems coaching candidates on common questions, generating scripted answers, and even handling initial video interviews
- **Spam Applications:** The volume of AI-assisted applications increased noise by 10x, making legitimate candidates harder to spot

**My Disadvantage:**
- I was competing against *both* well-qualified engineers *and* applicants using AI to artificially amplify their profiles
- The playing field was no longer merit-based—it was a game of keyword matching and application volume
- I didn't use aggressive AI tactics early on (philosophical choice), which meant I was outpaced by those who did

# My Specific Challenge: Interview Anxiety

Beyond the systemic issues, I had a personal gap: **I'm not naturally strong in interviews.**

**My Reality:**
- My portfolio speaks for itself: 25+ years across multiple tech stacks, leadership experience, successful product launches
- But in synchronous, high-pressure interview scenarios, I struggle to think on my feet
- I can't code to a timer well; I work best in flow states with time to think deeply
- Whiteboarding exercises and rapid-fire technical questions expose my weakness, not my strength

**Why This Mattered in 2024-2025:**
- Companies added **AI-assisted behavioral screening** and **automated coding challenges** before human interviews
- Candidates who bombed these automated tests never reached a human hiring manager
- My anxiety in interviews meant I likely failed these AI-filtered stages more often than peers
- By the time I reached a human, it was too late—I was already filtered out

## The Unemployment Collapse

**By September 2025, my unemployment benefits ran out.** I had been searching for 12 months without success.

**What Happened:**
- Job postings dried up through summer 2024 (hiring freeze)
- By fall 2024, postings resumed—but they required specific experience I didn't have or were filtered by ATS
- Winter 2024-2025: Market conditions worsened; competition remained fierce
- Spring/Summer 2025: Some recovery in hiring, but the damage to my timeline was done

**The Decision:** With unemployment exhausted and mounting financial pressure, I made the pragmatic choice to **transition into commercial truck driving**, a field where I could earn stable income immediately. I obtained my **Class A Commercial Driver's License (CDL) in July 2025**.

## Why This Period Was Uniquely Difficult

### For Someone with My Background:
1. **Overqualified & Underspecialized:** My diverse 25-year background didn't fit narrow ATS criteria
2. **Government Contract Liability:** SBA experience was too niche; companies assumed I'd jump ship for "real" work
3. **Timing:** Landed between two hiring cycles with maximum competition
4. **Age Factor:** Implicit concern that a 50+ engineer was expensive, inflexible, or near-retirement

### The Market Conditions:
1. **Structural Glut:** 250,000+ displaced tech workers competing for 5,000 open roles
2. **Automation Bias:** ATS favored pattern-matching over real competency assessment
3. **Uncertainty:** Companies paralyzed by AI's impact, unable to commit to hiring
4. **Cost Pressure:** Companies chose junior developers or outsourced work over senior hires

### My Personal Factors:
1. Interview anxiety that prevented me from reaching final decision-makers
2. Limited willingness to play aggressive ATS-gaming tactics
3. Geographic constraints (Oregon coast, seeking remote role)
4. Competing priorities (son's stability, financial runway)



## The Bottom Line

**October 2024 – October 2025 was a lost year in tech hiring.** It wasn't a reflection of my capabilities—my Makpar work delivered a production government portal on time, my technical skills remain current, and my track record demonstrates tangible impact. It was a convergence of:

- Macroeconomic correction eliminating stable contract work
- AI disruption freezing strategic hiring decisions  
- A 50-fold applicant surge for every open role
- Hiring systems optimized for filtration, not discovery
- My personal weakness in interview performance

By the time the market stabilized in late 2025, I had already committed to a different path. The job search experience, however, reinforced a critical lesson: **in a market dominated by automation and volume, the individual's agency matters less than systemic factors.**
